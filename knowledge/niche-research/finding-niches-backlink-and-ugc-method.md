## Smart work, not hard work

Think like the end user first: anyone building a real site also goes on to
build a Facebook page, a LinkedIn page, and brand it properly, the normal
sequence any legitimate business follows. That ordinary behaviour is the
shortcut technique A below turns into a discovery method — a brand-new site
betrays its own age by how recently it linked to Facebook, long before
anyone would otherwise notice it exists.

## A. Mining a giant's newest backlinks

The idea: instead of searching FOR niches directly, search for **who just
started acting like a real business** — and the earliest, most reliable
signal of that is a fresh link to Facebook, since setting up a page there is
one of the first things a new site owner does.

1. Open SEMrush and search **facebook.com**.
2. Go to **Backlinks** — this lists every site that links to Facebook, which
   at that scale is effectively "every site that has bothered to set up
   proper branding."
3. Check each linking site's **First Seen** and **Last Seen** dates — a site
   that both first and last linked to Facebook recently is a newly
   established site, not an old one that's been sitting there for years.
   These two dates together are what separate a brand-new competitor from
   background noise in a list that otherwise runs into the millions of rows.
4. Apply a **link placement** filter, then export the newly-established
   sites specifically — narrowing the export down to just the ones that
   matter, rather than the whole backlink list.
5. Hand the exported list to Claude (or another AI assistant) to analyze,
   then replicate whichever ones look promising.

This is a chore method in the sense that it takes going through a real list
rather than a single clever filter — but it's exactly the kind of list a
goldmine niche turns up in, precisely because almost nobody else is patient
enough to look through it.

## B. UGC sites as an intent-mismatch detector

Reddit and Quora are useful here as a detector, not a destination: if either
one shows up ranking for a keyword, that keyword has an **intent
mismatch** — what's actually ranking (a discussion thread) isn't what the
searcher wanted (a direct answer or download) — and that gap is the
opportunity, the same intent-mismatch principle covered in *Niche research —
tools, metrics, and selection criteria*. The logic is simple: Google only
ranks a forum thread for a query when nothing better already exists to
answer it, so a forum thread ranking well is itself proof that the gap is
still open.

To find these systematically:

1. Put a UGC site's domain (e.g. `reddit.com`) into SEMrush and open its
   **organic keywords** — the full list of queries that domain ranks for at
   all, across every subreddit and thread.
2. Apply an "include" filter — either a specific word (e.g. "download") or
   the 5W1H words (why, what, who, how, where) — to narrow that huge list
   down to queries that match the kind of niche worth building.
3. Any keyword in the results where Reddit or Quora is genuinely ranking is a
   candidate intent mismatch: a real, measurable search volume with no
   dedicated page currently satisfying it properly.

**Worked example:** searching "Roblox Apk Download" and finding an Amazon
page or a Reddit discussion thread ranking for it, instead of a direct
download page, is an intent mismatch — a searcher who typed that query wanted
a download, not a marketplace listing or a discussion about one, and the gap
between what they wanted and what's ranking is the goldmine.
