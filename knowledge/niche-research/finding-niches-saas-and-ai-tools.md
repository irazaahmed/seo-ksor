The marketplace-reverse-engineering method (see [Finding niches, Method 1](finding-niches-marketplaces.md))
extends to SaaS, AI tools, and apps too — it just needs a different set of
marketplaces, since Flippa and Empire Flippers skew toward content
sites.[^iskills-course-notes]

## Acquire.com

The SaaS equivalent of Flippa — a marketplace for buying and selling startups
and SaaS products.

1. Browse listings and screenshot ones that look promising.
2. Give the screenshot to ChatGPT or Claude and ask it to identify the
   product — its name or URL — or, if it can't, to describe a business that
   would replicate the same idea.
3. Once you have a URL (found or replicated), reverse-engineer it: check
   where its traffic actually comes from.

## Product Hunt

Browse for launched product ideas. As with Acquire.com, giving Product Hunt's
URL (or a specific listing) to an AI assistant and asking it to find
replicable products works as a shortcut to manual browsing.

## AppSumo

Browse lifetime-deal products. The useful signal here is in the **reviews**:
a product with a mix of 3–4 star reviews usually means real users exist but
have an unresolved complaint. That gap — the specific issue reviewers keep
raising — is the opportunity: build a version that fixes it, and it can be
sold on the same platform.

## Chrome Web Store

Browsing extensions surfaces a similar range of small, focused product ideas
worth validating the same way.

## Shopify app ideas

The Shopify app ecosystem is another source of product niches — ask an AI
assistant to help identify demand and gaps for a Shopify app idea before
building one.
